Executive Summary

The instalment journey works technically for most users, but several defects and gaps in clarity stop people from finishing it or leave them unsure about what they agreed to. Failures such as upload timeouts and mismatched amounts affect hundreds of journeys a month. The bigger problem is that users do not understand why income is asked, what a plan will cost them, or what happens after a missed payment. Those gaps push people to agents, who lack the context to help them, and broken plans end up in client reporting.

Thematic Synthesis
1. Technical Stability

The journey breaks at a few specific points, mostly on mobile and in data handling. Users lose progress or receive wrong information, and some of them drop out without any visible error.

Income document upload times out on mobile for large photos (Critical). 853 journeys in the sample month requested a document that never arrived, which makes this the largest single source of silent drop-off.
Portal amount differs from the letter (High). 531 journeys were affected in the sample month, 343 of them purchased-portfolio cases. Users refuse to agree until someone explains the difference (N-07).
Back button on plan review clears affordability answers (Medium). Users must re-enter sensitive financial information.
Acquired portfolios carry inconsistent data (Medium). Some lack reliable phone or address history (N-11), which limits what the journey can show or verify.
2. Onboarding & UX

The journey assumes a single claim, a clear income figure and a user who can judge the right plan alone. Users with several claims, irregular income or limited financial confidence struggle at the same points.

No way to combine multiple claims into one plan (High). Claims are shown one at a time, and users cannot tell whether paying one stops the letters about the other (N-03, T-202).
Ambiguous income question (High). Shift workers cannot tell whether “income” means this month or a normal month, and abandon the journey (N-01).
Shortest plan looks like the “right” answer (High). A user chose it, found the amount too high and missed the second payment (N-02). This links to the higher missed-payment rate seen after the plan order changed.
Mobile experience is weaker than desktop (Medium). Upload problems and drop-off concentrate on mobile, where most journeys start.
3. Trust & Clarity

Users hesitate because the journey does not explain itself. Not knowing why data is requested, what consequences follow or how amounts were calculated leads them to stop, avoid the digital channel or call instead.

No explanation of why income is asked or how long it is kept (High). Users fear the data will be used against them (N-06, T-206).
Consequences of a missed payment are not shown (High). This affects both the portal and the chat assistant, which confirmed a plan without explaining what happens after a late payment (N-05, T-208).
Letter and portal disagree on the amount (High). This erodes confidence in the digital channel (see Technical Stability).
Preference for human reassurance (Medium). Some users prefer to call because only a person can say whether a plan will cost more (N-04).
4. Support Load & Operational Visibility

Agents receive consumers who have already been through the digital journey, but cannot see what happened in it. The result is repeated questions, longer calls and no explanation when a plan fails.

Agent screen does not show what the consumer entered or why a plan was offered (High). Agents re-ask for details already typed online (N-08, N-09, T-205).
Broken plans are visible to clients through reporting (High). Clients weigh complaints as heavily as recovery (N-10).
Decisions are hard to explain (Medium). Compliance expects any recommendation to be explainable to both consumer and agent, and the current setup does not show how a decision was made (N-12).
